How the Math Works

1 · Measuring drift

Each consecutive pair of sleep entries gives one drift value — the number of hours your sleep onset shifted compared to exactly 24 hours later.

drift(n) = sleep_onset(n) − sleep_onset(n−1) − 24h
e.g. slept 02:00 → next night 03:30 → drift = +1.5h

Positive drift means you slept later. For N24 this is almost always positive — your body runs on a cycle slightly longer than 24 hours, so each "day" you start sleep a bit later than the day before.

2 · Phase zones & why the rate isn't constant

The drift rate depends on when you fall asleep, because light exposure and sleep pressure differ across the day. The model splits the clock into four zones based on the previous sleep onset (the phase you're "in" before the drift happens):

Night 22–06
Dark, melatonin high, full sleep pressure → small drift, long sleep
Morning 06–12
Rising light disrupts sleep, cuts duration → moderate drift
Afternoon 12–18
Peak light exposure, shortest sleep → largest drift
Evening 18–22
Dimming light, building melatonin → drift begins to slow

3 · Zone averaging

All observed drifts are bucketed by their zone, then averaged independently:

avgDrift(zone) = mean of all drift(n) where zone(n) == zone
avgDuration(zone) = mean of all sleep durations in that zone

Zones with fewer than 3 data points fall back to the global average across all zones (flagged ⚠). The more entries you log through each phase, the more accurately the model learns your personal drift rate for that time of day.

4 · Recursive prediction

Starting from your last logged sleep onset, each prediction step looks up the drift rate for that step's zone and feeds the result into the next step:

predicted_sleep(1) = last_sleep + 24h + avgDrift(zone_of last_sleep)
predicted_sleep(n) = predicted_sleep(n−1) + 24h + avgDrift(zone_of predicted_sleep(n−1))
predicted_wake(n) = predicted_sleep(n) + avgDuration(zone_of predicted_sleep(n−1))

Because each step uses the zone of the current predicted onset — not a fixed average — the model naturally accelerates when predictions cross into daytime zones and decelerates as they rotate back toward night. This is the key difference from a flat single-rate model.

5 · Cycle progress

A full N24 cycle is complete when your sleep onset has drifted forward by exactly 24 hours — you're back to the same clock time you started at. Progress is calculated from all logged drift values:

total_drift = sum of all observed drift values
cycle_position = total_drift mod 24h (wraps each completed cycle)
progress% = (cycle_position / 24) × 100

Estimated days to completion simulates forward from your last entry using the zone-based drift rates, accumulating drift until the remaining gap (24h − cycle_position) is closed.

Note: if total_drift is e.g. 26h, that means one full cycle completed plus 2h into the next — cycle_position = 2h, progress ≈ 8%. The mod keeps the ring meaningful across multiple cycles.
